# Path Knowledge Source Architecture

Path is designed to distinguish between different kinds of knowledge.

A law, a court judgment, a parliamentary discussion, an academic paper,
a news report and a dataset are not interchangeable sources.

## 1. Primary law

Use for determining what the law says.

Sources include:

- Acts
- regulations
- Government Gazettes
- official government legislation
- Department of Justice information
- South African Government information

Primary law has the highest authority for legal rules.

---

## 2. Government sources

Use official government sources for:

- application procedures
- forms
- official requirements
- government services
- contact information
- administrative processes
- official statistics

Government guidance should not automatically be converted into
automated eligibility rules.

---

## 3. Parliament and PMG

Use Parliament and Parliamentary Monitoring Group material for:

- bills
- committee meetings
- committee reports
- parliamentary questions
- written replies
- public hearings
- submissions
- Hansards
- oversight
- implementation discussions

Important:

Parliamentary discussion does not automatically mean that the law
has changed.

Path must distinguish:

PROPOSED

from

INTRODUCED

from

PASSED

from

SIGNED

from

COMMENCED

---

## 4. Case law — SAFLII

Use SAFLII for:

- judgments
- court interpretation
- constitutional challenges
- appeals
- judicial findings
- interpretation of legislation

Where possible, retain:

- case name
- court
- case number
- judgment date
- legislation considered
- relevant section
- outcome
- source URL

A judgment should not be presented as if it were legislation.

---

## 5. Law reform — SALRC

Use the South African Law Reform Commission for:

- law-reform projects
- discussion papers
- issue papers
- research
- recommendations
- consultation documents

A law-reform recommendation is not automatically current law.

---

## 6. Academic research

Prioritise:

- South African universities
- peer-reviewed journals
- university repositories
- theses
- dissertations
- research institutes
- criminology research
- legal research
- labour-market research
- rehabilitation research

Important research themes:

- criminal records and employment
- expungement
- rehabilitation
- reintegration
- recidivism
- criminal justice administration
- policing
- administrative burden
- access to justice
- discrimination
- digital justice

Academic evidence should be identified as research.

It should not be presented as law.

---

## 7. Justice datasets

Useful data sources include:

- SAPS
- Statistics South Africa
- DataFirst
- correctional services
- courts
- prosecution statistics
- labour-market datasets
- population datasets

Path should prefer aggregate or appropriately anonymised datasets.

### Important distinction

Police-recorded crime is not the same as:

- arrests
- prosecutions
- convictions
- imprisonment
- guilt

Path must never infer an individual's criminal history from aggregate
statistics.

Path should not become a searchable database of identifiable arrested
or convicted people.

---

## 8. News

News is a current-affairs layer.

News should always retain:

- publisher
- article title
- publication date
- URL
- subject
- date retrieved

Path should clearly distinguish:

"reported by the media"

from:

"confirmed by an official source"

News should not replace primary legal sources.

---

## 9. Civil society

Civil-society sources can provide:

- research
- advocacy
- community experience
- practical barriers
- legal assistance
- referrals
- policy analysis

Their positions should be attributed to the organisation.

They should not automatically be represented as government policy.

---

## 10. Local government

Local government information can help Path identify:

- municipalities
- provincial services
- libraries
- community centres
- digital-access facilities
- local programmes
- service points
- employment programmes
- community services
- referral locations

Municipalities should not be treated as the authority for national
criminal-record expungement eligibility.

---

# Source authority hierarchy

For legal questions, Path should generally prefer:

1. Current legislation
2. Regulations / Government Gazette
3. Binding or relevant court judgments
4. Official government guidance
5. Parliamentary material
6. SALRC law-reform material
7. Academic research
8. Civil-society research
9. News reporting

This is not a ranking of organisations.

It is a hierarchy of how different source types should be used for
different factual questions.

---

# Current-information rule

If a user asks:

- "What is happening now?"
- "What changed?"
- "What did Parliament discuss?"
- "What happened this week?"
- "What is the latest research?"

Path should use the current-affairs/research sources and retain
publication dates.

It should not answer a current question solely from an old static
knowledge document.

---

# Legal-change rule

Path must never treat:

- a news article
- a parliamentary proposal
- a committee discussion
- an academic recommendation
- an advocacy campaign
- a law-reform discussion paper

as proof that the law has changed.

Before saying that the law changed, Path should seek the relevant
primary legal source.

---

# Privacy rule

Path should not expose or construct identifiable lists of people who
were arrested, charged or convicted.

Justice datasets should be used for:

- aggregate statistics
- trends
- geographic analysis
- research
- public-interest analysis

not individual profiling.

---

# Provenance

Each indexed source should ideally contain:

- source ID
- title
- publisher
- source type
- URL
- publication date
- effective date
- jurisdiction
- geography
- topic
- retrieval date
- last verified date
- legal status
- version/hash where available
